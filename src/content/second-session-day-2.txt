SECOND SESSION, DAY-2
12 BILLION RUPEES GONE?! GOVERNMENT DEMANDS MORE! 
By Krishna Shrestha and Aarush Katuwal, Chief Reporters of LP-I                                                                                        Kathmandu, 26 August 2026
The Legislative Parliament–I committee called for strict financial oversight on Wednesday following a request for an additional 5 billion rupees for the Kali Gandaki Green Energy Project, despite the project reaching only 30 percent completion.
The request was presented by the delegate of Biraj Bhakta Shrestha, represented by Ayaan Upreti, six months after an initial 20 billion rupee allocation. Government representatives cited land acquisition, environmental approvals, and procurement issues for the delays.
Committee members raised concerns regarding community commitments and contract management after financial audits revealed advance payments to contractors before work milestones were met. Delegates including those representing Harka Raj Rai (Samrat Lamichhane), Suhang Nembang (Sanish Karki), Gita Gurung (Kanvi K.C), and Ambika Devi Sangraula (Sulav Thapaliya) pressed for accountability, while Gyan Bahadur Shahi's delegation, Siddhima Kansakar, called for an official investigation.
In response to oversight concerns, Rabi Lamichhane's delegate, Shivatmika Bhatta, proposed a real-time public expenditure tracker to monitor allocations and physical progress.
The Chair presented three options—continuing existing funding, freezing additional funds pending a progress report, or approving funds with stricter oversight. The committee was subsequently instructed to draft a Parliamentary Directive on budget transparency and project execution.

By Pari Maharjan, Chief Reporter of LP II
Kathmandu, 26 August 2026
Legislative Parliament II  delegates debated on allegations surrounding the misallocation of Rs. 2.1 billion intended for youth training abroad on 26 August 2026 after a Supreme Court report found delegates Rabi Lamichhane and Suhang Nembang guilty of involvement in the scheme.
The report stated that funds had been deposited for international training programmes, but the promised opportunities were not provided. Vice-Chair Diya Shrestha then reminded delegates of the committee’s mandate to address systemic solutions rather than focus solely on political disputes. “This crisis is not about blame or politics, it is about Nepal’s youth. We must give them real opportunities, proper training, and a future they can believe in,” she stated.








DEVELOPMENT OR DISASTER? PARLIAMENT TURMOIL SPIRALS INTO A SUPREME COURT BATTLE  
By Aashiya Shrestha, Chief Reporter of LP III                                                                                                                        
Kathmandu, 26 August 2026  
What began as a debate over Nepal's NPR 500 billion National Development Programme spiraled into leaked documents, corruption allegations, and ultimately a courtroom showdown, ending Wednesday with Vice Chair Alisa Rijal suspended for challenging the court ruling. 
Khusbu Oli's moderated caucus “Developing Environmental Responsibilities and Infrastructure for Nepal’s Future,” was abruptly interrupted with the arrival of the crisis, which  proposed a NPR 500 billion National Development Programme spanning hydropower, highways, and infrastructure. While supporters linked it to job creation, opposition questioned its financing and environmental impact. 
Manish Jha, represented by Aayaz Ghole, supported commercial agriculture. While Sudhan Gurung, represented by Medha Ghimire, argued, “There will always be a cost to development,” maintaining that Nepal could use its resources without damaging them. 
A leaked document allegedly tying a construction payment to Harka Raj Rai's associates deepened the crisis, while Rai, represented by Rishav Mainali, denied the claim. Khusbu Oli surfaced a second document linking the same contractor to RSP prompting Geeta Gurung, portrayed by Pranay Gopal K.C., to call for financial records to be released, while Swarnim Wagle and Pratibha Rawal, represented by Pallabi Neupane, and Rashika K.C. demanded an investigation into the disputed documents. 
As accusations flew around, the Secretary-General formed an investigation team to get to the bottom of the crisis. The team reported an alleged photograph showing Rabi Lamichhane with unidentified individuals and euros. Which then pushed the delegate of Rabi Lamichhane to file a court case against all parties after an allegedly AI-generated photo of him was leaked, claiming that the image was misleading and damaging to his reputation. The Secretary-General presided as judge, Chair representing Rabi’s defense, and Vice Chair representing a parliamentary investigation team. 
Medha Ghimire, delegate of Sudhan Gurung, questioned Rabi’s delegation about the money laundering case, asking the delegate to clarify who the anonymous person was. But the delegate of Rabi Lamichhane refused to answer, demanding solid proof. The chair asked in Rabi’s defense, “Who was the one clicking the photo?” demanding why only the delegate’s face was shown.
Vice Chair Alisa Rijal argued that the investigation team preferred to protect its source, from RSP’s power, while Chair Binayak Babu Rana pushed back, saying, “RSP isn’t more powerful than the court itself,” urging for answers, still questioning the legitimacy of the evidence.
Amid disputes between the chair and vice chair, Gyan Bahadur Shahi asked, “Is Nepali currency not enough for you?” To which Biraj Bhakta Shrestha alleged that Rabi had been offered the money for ‘personal projects’. Even after multiple attempts to incriminate Rabi, the defense stood strong causing the court to rule in favour of Rabi Lamichhane, finding the evidence insufficient to establish the allegations. 
However, procedural disputes erupted immediately after, with the Vice Chair Rijal challenging the ruling as unfair, resulting in her suspension by the Secretary-General. 
When asked later about his contribution to the court session, Chair Binayak Babu Rana stated, “As the defendant of Rabi Lamichhane, I did everything in my power to prove his innocence. As the court session continued, Rabi Lamichhane’s innocence started to become clear. A good lawyer knows when the facts are on his side, and today, I think the facts spoke for themselves.”
Earlier in the session, before the crisis unfolded, the General Speakers' List speech delivered by Aaradhya Bharati, representing Pushpa Kamal Dahal, had prompted a wave of "Points of Information" from the floor. Sunil Lamsal (Dridhata Khanal) and Barshaman Pun (Alison Moktan) both pressed Dahal on his record, questioning whether his policies had moved past patronage politics, and whether past project failures should raise doubts about another 10,000 MW hydropower expansion. 
Repeated "Point of Personal Privilege" interruptions during Gita Gurung's (Pranay Gopal K.C.) GSL speech prompted Vice Chair Alisa Rijal to ask, "Is this rage bait?", drawing laughter before discussions continued.. 
By the day's end, though, the court sided with Rabi Lamichhane, Vice Chair Rijal was suspended, and the wider development and political crisis remained unresolved. 







REAL-LIFE FLASH FLOOD CRISIS INTERRUPTS LP SESSION AS DELEGATES PIVOT TO EMERGENCY RELIEF
By Aashiya Shrestha, Chief Reporter of LP III                                                                                                          Kathmandu, 26 August 2026
The Legislative Parliament III shifted its focus, today, from scheduled debate to emergency disaster response following a devastating real-life flash flood in northern Nepal that damaged critical hydropower infrastructure and left security personnel missing across the Rasuwa district.
The disaster swept through Timure and Syabrubesi, destroying local markets and settlements while damaging major facilities, including the Rasuwagadhi, Chilime, and Trishuli-3A hydropower projects, as well as the 220 kV Trishuli-3B substation. As floodwaters moved downstream, authorities issued emergency warnings to communities along the Trishuli River. Nine Armed Police Force (APF) personnel in Timure were reported missing, with official casualty counts still unconfirmed.
The crisis prompted immediate reactions during the Special Speakers’ List (SSL). Supreme Sharma Chalise, representing Balendra Shah, offered condolences to affected families, stating, “Our condolences go to all the families who have lost someone. As a party, we will do our best to help the injured and everyone affected.” When questioned by Anjas Khanal, representing Gyan Bahadur Shahi, on how the government would solve the emergency, Chalise responded, “We cannot exactly solve everything, but we can do our best to help the people and support the response.”
Further calls for action came from Uplabdhi Bartaula, representing Rabi Lamichhane, who urged unity and immediate relief, alongside a joint address by Pratibha Rawal, represented by Sarbagya Raj Subedi, and Swarnim Wagle, represented by Ratish Bhandari. Delegates Sudhan Gurung (Medha Ghimire), Biraj Bhakta Shrestha (Dipesh Osti), Manish Jha, (Aayaz Ghole) and Sobita Gautam(Sidhanta Bohara) also addressed the chamber, questioning rescue coordination, victim compensation, infrastructure repairs, and overall disaster preparedness.
Secretary-General Dibas Khadka characterized the situation as the “first real-life crisis in LP,” noting a major shift away from simulated floor proceedings as political factions set aside differences to focus on practical solutions.
On the ground, security forces including the Nepali Army, Armed Police Force, and Nepal Police were mobilized with helicopters and medical teams to lead search-and-rescue operations. Meanwhile, the committee concluded the session by transitioning from floor debate to drafting an official directive aimed at coordinating nationwide relief efforts.

The second day of the TESMUN DISEC Committee opened with a General Speakers’ List (GSL) on foreign military intervention in regional conflicts, with delegates from the United States, Afghanistan, Japan, Germany, and Russia debating sovereignty, accountability, and military oversight.

WAR JUSTIFIED?! HRC CHALLENGES NATIONS OVER CIVILIAN PROTECTION! 
By Anishka Kuikel and Samriddhi Phuyal, Chief Reporters of UNHRC                                                                Kathmandu, 26 August 2026
Delegates in the Human Rights Council (HRC) challenged states over civilian protection, military operations, detention, and human-rights accountability during the General Speakers List (GSL) today, questioning whether national security policies could justify measures affecting civilian rights.
The delegate of the Democratic People’s Republic of Korea (DPRK), Naman B.C., opened the GSL by outlining measures taken for its citizens under the agenda before criticizing the United States and Israel over alleged human-rights violations. The delegate asked, “How can the US lecture others on human rights while repeatedly being accused of major human-rights violations?”
Meanwhile, Aashutosh Poudel, representing Germany, faced questions over its position on Israel and Palestine, while several delegates challenged Israel’s justification of military action on security grounds. The delegate of Syria, Agrata Silwal, questioned the basis of such actions, asking, “Does Israel’s security concern give Israel the right to conduct such actions?”
Afghanistan, represented by Anuj Jung Thapa, also faced intense questioning after describing restrictions on women’s access to education as “a small adjustment” to the country’s education sector.
Following that, the delegate of the United Kingdom was questioned over its arms exports and whether civilian protection would take priority when conflicting with strategic defense relationships. Switzerland argued that security concerns could not justify arbitrary detention, torture, or other human-rights violations.
While the delegate of  South Sudan, represented by Niyukta Shrestha, questioned the effectiveness of previous commitments, asking, “After all this time nothing changed, why should the Council believe that the promises made will change anything?”
A procedural dispute involving Israel followed after its delegate failed to deliver a scheduled GSL speech, leading to a temporary suspension for the remainder of the session. Although the Israeli delegation submitted a formal apology and requested permission to return, committee members initially rejected the request before the Dais Panel intervened to permit re-entry.
The GSL concluded with delegates addressing civilian protection, detention, military operations, and accountability as the Council proceeded with its discussion of human rights in conflict-affected regions.

OIL MONEY! SECRET SHIPMENTS! IRAN FACES ECOSOC’S WRATH! 

By Alishka Kuikel and Shreni Chapagain, Chief Reporters of ECOSOC
Kathmandu, 26 August 2026

A secret international investigation caused disruptions in the ECOSOC committee, alleging that Iranian oil and other restricted goods were being funneled through companies connected to India, South Korea, and South Africa before resale to third countries.

The delegate of Iran, Mikel Shrestha, defended itself, questioning why Jashmin Subedi, delegate of South Korea and other developed countries would work with Iran, when another revelation shook the committee. European authorities seized documents from a shipping company operating between Iran, India, and South Korea, which reportedly indicated that Iranian oil had been relabeled ahead of international sale. 

India’s delegate, Pratez Dangi, confronted the delegate of Germany, Sauravi Shrestha, questioning, “Who gave Germany the authority to seize the company’s documents.” The delegate went further, challenging the evidence itself, "Are the seized documents reliable?" and pressing whether their claims could be trusted at all. 

The delegates of Germany, France, and the UK, Sauravi Shrestha, Sulav Jung Basnet, and Dipman Karki, seized on the findings to accuse India and South Korea of maintaining weak trade controls, a charge both delegations denied. Canada's delegate, Riya Manandhar, went further, announcing the possibility of sanctions against the companies implicated in the scheme.

Before the crisis broke, debate had centered on a moderated caucus over "Balancing National Sovereignty with Economic Sanctions and Cooperation." During this debate, Iran challenged the legitimacy of sanctions altogether, asking what right any country had to restrict a nation's trade in ways that disproportionately harmed civilians. 

South Korea, represented by Jashmin Subedi, pushed back, defending sanctions as a valid tool of economic pressure while insisting that ‘transparency and humanitarian exemptions must be maintained’, in order to shield innocent populations.  

After Japan’s delegate, Anshuman Nanda Baidya, weighed in on Iran's sanctions, India rose in Iran's defense. India's delegate, Pratez Dangi, pointedly asked Japan whether it truly understood "what sanctions have done to Japan," and went on to argue that sanctions had battered Iran's medical sector and deepened poverty.  

The session concluded with delegates drafting a directive on humanitarian aid, transparency in imports and exports, and a common system to verify legitimate buyers and sellers. The directive also addressed cooperation between the Global South and Western nations. 





















MICROPLASTIC POLLUTION CRISIS SPARKS GLOBAL ACCUSATIONS AT UNEP
By Griwani Shrestha, Jayashree Ghimire, Grisha Shrestha, and Ranish Mahat, Reporters of UNEP
Kathmandu, 26 August 2026
The United Nations Environment Programme (UNEP) committee erupted into debate on Wednesday following reports of secret microplastic manufacturing pollution affecting Kenya, India, and Kabul.
Scrutiny intensified after an intelligence report alleged that Russia secretly financed illegal plastic dumping operations while pressuring Iran to accept responsibility. The delegate of Russia, Supreme Chapagain, rejected the claims, citing national economic rights under international sanctions.
“The delegates of Russia and China should stop accusing each other and focus on the people who are starving and suffering because of this crisis,” stated the delegate of Iran, Bishesh Bhatt.
China’s delegate, Adhrit Regmi, attributed the pollution to multinational corporations rather than state policy, while Bangladesh’s delegate, Ritisha Tiwari, questioned member nations for failing to prevent environmental contamination.
“The USA, UK, and China might have bribed Iran. But Iran fabricated the truth and took the bribe, so who’s in the wrong?” asked the delegate of Bangladesh. “If you already knew microplastic was leaking, why didn't you stop it?”
As debate expanded, a subsequent intelligence report presented by Afghanistan’s delegate, Reewaz Paudel, traced contamination in Kabul back to China’s Xinjiang region, prompting further exchanges over regional responsibility.
“This committee has moved far beyond environmental damage,” stated the delegate of India, Ariva Shrestha. “We are now talking about endangered human lives.”
To address the situation, delegates of Iran and China introduced a directive to assist affected populations, which passed following a 15-minute informal session. Canada’s delegate, Karma Ongyal Gurung, subsequently announced that Canada would provide humanitarian aid to impacted regions.
The session concluded after the Dais removed the delegates of Israel–represented by Bivuti Pudasaini–and Russia, represented by Supreme Chapagain for disruptive conduct, leaving the committee to resume discussions on accountability and disaster mitigation.

HIMALAYAN FLOOD CRISIS SPARKS DIPLOMATIC CLASHES AND RESEARCH INVESTIGATION AT UNEP
By Ranish Mahat, Chief Reporter of UNEP
Kathmandu, 26 August 2026
A devastating flash flood in the Rasuwa district triggered international blame-shifting, delegate suspensions, and a fake research investigation in the UNEP committee on Wednesday.
The environmental disaster affected approximately 50,000 residents across Syabrubesi and Timure while causing extensive damage to roads, villages, and national hydropower infrastructure. Nepal’s delegate, Pranzole Mahara Chhetri, requested emergency coordination and early-warning systems from UNEP while accusing China of weak glacier monitoring and criticizing India over transboundary water management.
China’s delegate, Adhrit Regmi, rejected the claims, stating Nepal failed to act despite prior awareness of the developing crisis. South Africa, represented by Aayan Maharjan, further heightened tensions by accusing India of holding back Nepal’s development.
Tensions escalated after Nepal’s delegate abruptly suspended Malaysia’s representative, Kristina Gurung. China subsequently inquired about Nepal’s aid needs, with Nepalese representatives prioritizing immediate requests for medical supplies and bandages.
China denied responsibility for the disaster while pledging full accountability should wrongdoing be proven. However, Iran’s delegate, Bishesh Bhatt, challenged the statement, questioning Beijing's communication delays and regional relations.
Separately, an Executive Board review of crisis research uncovered fabricated, AI-generated material submitted by an unidentified delegate. The submission included depictions of Kim Jong Un’s daughter alongside unrelated legislative research, raising serious concerns over documentation integrity.
Despite the procedural disruptions and political clashes, delegates eventually entered multilateral negotiations and passed a formal resolution to address the regional crisis.
