MP-I
27 CHILDREN MISSING: SYSTEMIC FAILURES PROMPT EMERGENCY 'OPERATION SAFE CHILD' PROBE 
By Krishna Shrestha and Aarush Katuwal, Chief Reporters of LP-I
Kathmandu, 2 September 2026
The second day of the third session of the TESMUN Legislative Parliament descended into a national crisis on Wednesday, as the sudden disappearance of 27 children across six regions exposed widespread institutional miscommunication, forcing Prime Minister Balendra Shah’s administration to launch "Operation Safe Child."
The crisis unfolded as law enforcement pivoted from treating the disappearances as isolated cases to investigating training schools and recruitment agencies as central vectors of the scheme. "Families were promised education and job opportunities for their children," noted lead investigators, raising serious concerns that victims were funneled into forced labor networks or human trafficking rings.
Under the new directive, the Prime Minister ordered five key ministries—Home, Labour, Education, Women and Children, and Health—to break departmental silos and pool intelligence to locate the missing children.
The institutional breakdown was presciently foreshadowed earlier in the day during a moderated caucus on child protection, where delegates delivered an unorthodox, out-of-tune musical performance to satirize government agencies for failing to communicate with one another.
Search warrants executed at a target recruitment agency quickly uncovered falsified placement records, placing several top cabinet officials under immediate scrutiny. The Ministry of Labour faced criticism for failing to monitor unverified job programs, while the Ministry of Education was questioned over unverified third-party communications directed at minors, and the Ministry of Women and Children was faulted for ignoring cross-departmental missing person reports.
Pressure intensified further on Home Affairs Minister Sudhan Gurung, represented by Prasanna Adhikari, who was summoned before Parliament to address systemic delays after families confirmed raising red flags prior to the disappearances. "Why did the department fail to act when families warned of suspicious recruitment beforehand?" lawmakers demanded during a tense parliamentary inquiry.
As evidence mounts linking recruitment networks to private medical groups and financial kickbacks, Operation Safe Child has shifted the debate from isolated administrative errors to an urgent demand for structural government reform.

MP-III
‘THE DISEASE IS AMONG YOU’: THE KIDNAPPING THAT SHOOK PARLIAMENT
By Chief Reporter of LP III, Aashiya Shrestha
2 September 2026
A minister vanished. A mysterious file surfaced. And suddenly, Parliament had more questions than answers. What began as Swarnim Wagle's corruption investigation soon spiraled into a kidnapping crisis that transformed the entire session.
During the crisis, Swarnim Wagle, represented by Pallabi Neupane, had announced an investigation into corruption, suspicious contracts and misuse of public money. But before the delegate could reveal his findings, he disappeared.
Swarnim Wagle’s delegate, Pallabi Neupane, then portrayed his personal assistant, blaming the opposition for the disappearance. While Khusbu Oli, represented by Rajashwi Pradhan, questioned, “How are you sure the opposition kidnapped Wagle?” The chair then stepped in with pointed questions, “Where were you when Wagle was kidnapped?”, questioning how a Finance Minister disappeared despite security.
In response, Pallabi Neupane, as the personal assistant, said he had been with Gyan Bahadur Shahi, prompting the chair to ask why Wagle himself had been with Shahi in the first place. Harka Raj Rai's delegate, Rishav Mainali, came to Neupane's defense, arguing that a personal assistant is 'not a security guard. 
The debate escalated as Sudhan Gurung, represented by Medha Ghimire, questioned Wagle’s alliance, while Rabi Lamichhane, represented by Uplabdhi Bartaula, argued that deeper problems were being hidden. This prompted Biraj Bhakta Shrestha’s delegation, Dipesh Osti, to break decorum, telling Rabi to “shut up.”
Investigators soon recovered part of Wagle’s database containing suspicious contracts, unexplained spendings and political appointments. One section was labelled “R.G.H.”, but its meaning remained unknown. Suhang Nembang, represented by Divya Pudasaini, and later Sudhan Gurung offered different theories about “R.G.H.”, but neither was confirmed.
A leaked document allegedly linked Gyan Bahadur Shahi to financial irregularities. Rabi called it fabricated, while Wagle’s team defended it, saying it came from the original files. Then an anonymous video showed Wagle alive. He warned, “The people protecting it are the disease.” The video ended before he could reveal more. Investigators also discovered someone inside his team had deleted evidence.
Police later found the building where Wagle was allegedly held and a hidden device containing his final corruption database. CCTV also showed a vehicle connected to someone close to Harka at the kidnapping location. Gyan Bahadur Shahi, Rabi Lamichhane and Harka Raj Rai denied involvement.
Suddenly, Wagle’s phone sent every delegate a chilling message: “The people who ordered my silence are sitting among you.” The phone immediately went offline, sending Parliament into chaos.
With trust collapsing, RSP demanded Rabi’s resignation, causing the delegation to step down. This created a leadership crisis and led to a fresh RSP chairperson election. Sobita Gautam was proposed while Sudhan contested, but at last Biraj Bhakta Shrestha won.
The Prime Minister vote followed, Sudhan receiving zero votes, Arjun one, while Pushpa Kamal Dahal, represented by Aaradhya Bharati, won with 15 votes. Dahal promised reforms, accountability and zero corruption. Delegates stood and applauded after his speech, giving an unexpected salute.
Secretary-General Dibas Khadka closed the session, saying Model Parliament was beginning to resemble a real Parliament and reminding delegates that they still had to work together.
From a missing minister to secret files, heated debates and new leadership, the crisis changed Parliament completely. Wagle may have disappeared, but his investigation left Parliament with a truth it could no longer ignore.

Kathmandu, 2 September 2026
What began as a routine debate on stabilizing global energy supplies spiraled into allegations of hypocrisy, economic sabotage, and the threat of armed conflict on Wednesday, as ECOSOC careened toward a major diplomatic showdown following the United States' announcement of a new military initiative, "Mission Blue Moon."

The session's tone had already been tense well before the crisis broke. The debate had opened with the delegate of Argentina, Shayana Khakurel,  challenging Ethiopia's credibility on the issue, arguing, "How can Ethiopia speak of global energy stabilization when its large population still lacks global energy?" Ethiopia’s delegate, Shara June Maharjan, responded that it was "advocating towards developing an equal domestic gap," while Argentina maintained that energy security must be based on reliability, affordability and international cooperation.
The debate then turned toward the USA's role in the energy crisis. South Korea’s delegation, Jashmin Subedi, argued that while the USA continues on expanding fossil fuels for profit, it simultaneously "supports clean energy" pointing out that the country’s claim and actions contradict.
Iran’s delegate, Mikel Shrestha, gained the spotlight after the accusations thrown by  the EU bloc and UK. The delegate pushed back, questioning, “What is the point of aid when the delegate of Iran cannot have the freedom. When Iran cannot use its own resources?”
India, represented by Pratez Dangi, echoed the criticism, challenging the USA, UK, France, and Canada directly, "When leaders cannot lead developing countries then how can humanitarians still exist?", later urging fellow delegates to "open your eyes."

Ayushman Dahal, accused the nation of failing its citizens, claiming 43% of its population lacks access to humanitarian aid. The DPRK fired back, accusing the EU of following the USA "like a herd of sheep” afraid of the consequences of opposition.

Amid the escalating exchanges, the dais was notably frustrated, repeatedly calling the committee to order, and urging delegates to move beyond circular arguments and focus on constructive resolution. 
The committee's call for energy stabilization was overshadowed by the looming threat of broader conflict, as the crisis update pushed delegates back toward urgent discussions on securing energy supplies and addressing the crisis's wider global effects.





















UNEP
BILLIONS, BLAME & A BURNING PLANET: UNEP’S GREEN PROMISE UNDER FIRE
By Ranish Mahat, Chief Reporter of UNEP                                                                                                                 Kathmandu, 2 September 2026
Delegates at the United Nations Environment Programme (UNEP) clashed over resource consumption and international supply chains on Wednesday, as a major artificial intelligence project in the UAE sparked intense courtroom litigation over environmental damage. 
The session opened with a debate on balancing technological growth with ecosystem protection. Iranian Delegate Bishesh Bhatt warned that development must not compromise basic human access to water and food. Concerns mounted as Afghan Delegate Reewaz Paudel highlighted fossil fuel dependence, while Chinese Delegate Adhrit Regmi urged UNEP to enforce strict global standards for sustainable infrastructure. 
The committee shifted into full crisis mode following revelations surrounding a massive tech development in the United Arab Emirates. UAE Delegate Arpita Lamichhane defended the facility as essential for economic diversification, despite environmental reports revealing it consumes 20 million liters of water daily and raised local power demand by 18%. 
Tensions escalated as investigations tied international suppliers to the facility's footprint. While UNEP confirmed Chinese suppliers offered advanced cooling technology that could cut water usage by 25%, the UAE selected a cheaper conventional system. China maintained the final decision rested with the host country, while the UAE argued that replacing the technology would cost billions and cause years of delay. 
The supply chain investigation quickly implicated multiple nations. Russian Delegate Supreme Chapagain acknowledged supplying batteries but denied knowledge of their final application, while Germany’s Delegate Yudin Thapa Magar defended his country’s engineering firms against accusations of inadequate oversight raised by Delegate of Malaysia, Kristina Gurung. Meanwhile, Bangladesh’s Delegate Ritisha Tiwari demanded to know why suppliers failed to perform proper inspections after refurbished water technology was routed through regional trade networks.
The dispute culminated in a high-stakes courtroom session led by advocates Rujal Ojha and Unnat Aryal. While defense counsel argued that replacing equipment would cause unsustainable financial strain, opposing counsel maintained that saving money cannot justify severe environmental destruction when viable alternatives exist.
Following presidential addresses and heated exchanges that led to temporary delegate suspensions, the court ultimately delivered its verdict, ruling in favor of the coalition backed by Bangladesh and the UAE.
