SECOND SESSION, DAY-1


WHO FAILED NEPAL?! GEN Z PROTESTS PUT GOVERNMENT ACCOUNTABILITY ON TRIAL! 

-By Aarush Katuwal and Krishna Shrestha, Chief Reporters of LP-I
Kathmandu, 25 August 
A debate over Nepal’s federal structure quickly turned into a confrontation over the Gen Z protests as delegates clashed over government accountability, political responsibility, and the actions of leaders.
The session opened with a General Speakers List (GSL) speech from logistics team member Yugesh Adhikari, who appeared as a shadow delegate portraying former Prime Minister K.P. Sharma Oli. Addressing the committee, he warned, "The Parliament cannot function when political posturing replaces our duty; we must rearrange our focus on good, structural governance." His GSL speech ultimately earned him "Good GSL" recognition from the Executive Board.
The debate intensified as opposition delegates questioned the government's handling of unallocated municipal funds, administrative delays, and accountability within Nepal's federal structure. Delegates representing Gyan Bahadur Shahi, Rabi Lamichhane, Ambika Thapa, Shishir Khanal, Gita Gurung, and Sasmit Pokhrel also raised sharp concerns regarding the government's response to the Gen Z protests.
The delegate of Gyan Bahadur Shahi, portrayed by Siddhima Kansakar, accused the administration of utilizing procedural delays to avoid critical debate. Meanwhile, the delegation of Rabi Lamichhane directly challenged the government’s moral standing during the unrest, stating, "A government that stands passive while its youth protest and locks up political opponents during a protest has lost its moral authority to speak on federal accountability."
Defending the administration, the delegate of Balendra Shah, portrayed by Ayub Lamsal, argued that the second agenda should focus on strengthening local governance rather than revisiting past political disputes. He stated that effective federalism required "systematic execution" and warned that arguments over the protests should not derail legislative progress.
The government's position received support from delegates including Sobita Gautam, represented by Aashvi Chalise, Pushpa Kamal Dahal, represented by Sabuj Jung Dhakal, and Home Minister Sudan Gurung, represented by Prasanna Adhikari. Sudan Gurung argued that accountability must apply equally across all levels of government, asserting, "Accountability cannot be established through selective outrage; law enforcement and institutional integrity apply equally across all tiers of governance."
As tensions continued, the committee attempted to move the discussion toward concrete reforms. Resolution 1.1 proposed passing the delayed Federal Civil Service Act within 60 days, introducing biannual audits of user committees through the Office of the Auditor General, and reactivating the Intergovernmental Coordination Council to improve coordination between federal and local governments.
However, repeated interjections and disruptions prevented the committee from maintaining focus. Chair Aakarshi Paudel, Vice-Chair Dilasha Oli, and Moderator Sijan Neupane eventually suspended proceedings.
"The purpose of this committee is policy resolution, not unparliamentary chaos," the Chair warned as the Dias Panel issued a final warning against further desk-banging and other unparliamentary conduct.
What began as a debate over federal governance had ultimately transformed into a wider confrontation over the government's response to the Gen Z protests.





















PARLIAMENT AT A BREAKING POINT! DELEGATES CLASH OVER YOUTH MIGRATION! 

-By Pari Maharjan, Chief Reporter of LP-II 
Kathmandu, 25 August 
Delegates of Legislature Parliament II challenged government officials over persistent youth unemployment, foreign labor migration, and a lack of formal jobs on 25 August in the parliament, demanding verifiable proof that state employment policies are delivering results. 
The debate opened with opposition representatives targeting government-funded job programs. Prasanna Puri, representing Rabi Lamichhane, criticized Tshering Dolma Gurung, delegate of Pushpa Kamal Dahal, over the administration’s employment programs.
"Despite significant government investment, the initiatives have failed to generate enough employment opportunities," Puri stated on behalf of Lamichhane.
Government representatives pushed back, arguing that structural economic challenges require long-term solutions. Evesh K.C., representing Barsha Man Pun, defended the administration's framework alongside Gurung.
"A problem built over generations cannot be resolved through a single programme," Gurung responded on behalf of Pushpa Kamal Dahal.
The discussion then shifted to the question of actual job creation. Manjul Pratap Ghimire, representing Balendra Shah, called for concrete statistics on job creation figures.
"More than 8,00,000 Nepalis are seeking opportunities abroad," noted Gaurav Dahal, representing Gyan Bahadur Sahi, while challenging the Rastriya Swatantra Party (RSP) to present actionable alternatives.
The focus subsequently turned to reviving domestic manufacturing. Balendra Shah highlighted the inactive Hetauda textile industry as a key unused capacity.
"How quickly can new employment be generated when major industries have remained closed for decades?" the delegate of Rabi Lamichhane questioned in response, while Somiya Gupta, representing Suhang Nembang, asked how proposed jobs would reach those most in need.
The committee also examined historical records, job quality, and regional solutions. Anjali Bhandari, representing Sobita Gautam, cited the 2,973 minors identified among former Maoist combatants during UN verification to challenge Pushpa Kamal Dahal's commitment to youth.
"Will the expansion of Nepal’s tourism sector create secure careers or mainly seasonal, low-paid jobs?" delegate of Gauri Kumari, represented by Nizma Maharjan, questioned.
Yuvika Adhikari, representing Harka Raj Rai, proposed developing industries around local resources to retain skilled workers. The debate concluded with delegates divided over whether current strategies can deliver measurable employment outcomes for Nepal's youth population.




























POWER! POLITICS! DEBATE EXPLODES OVER GREEN FUTURE! 
-By Aashiya Shrestha, Chief Reporter of LP III
Kathmandu, 25 August 

Escalating tension over Nepal’s energy and agricultural plans dominated Legislature Parliament III on August 25, as delegates cross-examined party representatives on the ecological costs of rapid industrial growth.
The General Speakers List (GSL) opened with an immediate clash regarding Nepal's huge potential for clean energy development. Rishav Mainali, representing Harka Raj Rai, challenged Sulav Ghimire, delegate of Shishir Khanal, demanding a clear strategy to break Nepal's dependence on foreign imports.
"Nepal should reduce dependency by producing more domestically, strengthening agriculture and industries, expanding clean energy, and increasing exports," Ghimire declared on behalf of Khanal.
The discussion then moved to RSP’s development plans. Harka Raj Rai questioned Manish Jha, represented by Aayaz Ghole, on how RSP would ensure sustainable development. Jha replied, "We need development, but not at the cost of our environment. RSP would focus on proper environmental assessments, transparency, and accountability."
After a successful motion by Rai, the committee shifted into a Moderated Caucus on "Balancing Development and Environmental Protection through Sustainable Government in Nepal." Turning the spotlight on state infrastructure, Harka Raj Rai pressed opposition delegates to explain how Nepal could realistically meet its ambitious 28,000 MW power-development target by 2035 without a foundational power grid in place.
Shishir Khanal counter-argued by highlighting Nepal's massive 72,544 MW gross hydropower potential, igniting a debate over whether national institutions possess the operational capacity to build required projects. Harka Raj Rai’s sharp line of questioning ultimately earned him a rare "point to be noted" recognition from Committee Chair, Binayak Babu Rana.
The session concluded with an examination of party integrity and sector-specific reform. Dipesh Osti, representing Biraj Bhakta Shrestha, strongly criticized established political parties, prompting applause from delegates across the floor.
"How can a party promise positive growth if corruption, disadvantage, and other political debates remain?" asked Uplabdhi Bartaula, representing Rabi Lamichhane, while confronting CPN-UML representatives.
Closing arguments brought further friction, as Pallabi Neupane, representing Swarnim Wagle, probed agricultural modernization policies in Ilam, while Rashika K.C., representing Pratibha Rawal, outlined severe labor and engineering constraints facing conservation efforts.





























46 DEAD! MISSILE ATTACK PUSHES DISEC TO THE BRINK! 

-By Shubham Shrestha and Osang Ghising,  Chief Reporters of DISEC
Kathmandu, 25 August 

A turn of events in the DISEC committee led to delegates debating the role of foreign military intervention in regional conflicts on Tuesday, with accusations over sovereignty, cross-border attacks and military involvement. 

During the unmoderated caucus which was centered on the agenda “Examining the Role of Foreign Military Intervention in the Escalation of Regional Conflicts,” several delegations directly challenged one another over their involvement in ongoing conflicts. Saya Luitel,the representative of Israel, accused Lebanon, represented by Arpita Maharjan, of violating its sovereignty through cross-border attacks, arguing that continued aggression could further destabilize the region.

The accusations sparked many exchanges, as delegates questioned whether foreign intervention was preventing conflicts from being contained or instead pushing them toward escalation. The delegate of Iran, Soval Kunwar, also came under heavy criticism from several delegations, with opposing delegates raising concerns over its role in regional instability and alleged support for armed groups.

The delegation of the US, represented by Aashima Bhattarai exclaimed, ”Belarus is just a shadow of Russia. It should just sign its sovereignty away and bring back the Soviet Union.”

The delegation of China, represented by Sadikshya Bhattarai, became increasingly involved in the debate, while the delegate of the United States directed accusations toward France over its alleged role in regional military affairs. The delegate of Israel further argued that continued foreign involvement was adding “fuel to the fire,” intensifying tensions between states rather than contributing to diplomatic solutions.

The committee was then thrown into crisis when reports emerged of a missile attack that dramatically increased tensions between China and Japan. The attack reportedly resulted in 46 civilian deaths and the deaths of three Israeli military commanders, immediately raising concerns of retaliation and further escalation. The delegate of China claimed that the missiles contained Russian components, introducing another layer of international involvement into the developing crisis.

The delegation of Russia, represented by Sabhya Devkota, argued, ”The US is responsible for the bombing of Hiroshima and Nagasaki during WWII, so on what basis can we be sure that the delegate of the USA is on the same side as Japan?”

As the situation deteriorated, Saya Luitel, Shanvi Rajbhandari, and Sadikshya Bhattarai , delegates of Israel, Japan and China emerged as central figures in the growing confrontation, with tensions rising as the countries prepared for possible retaliation. Delegates were forced to shift their focus from conventional debate toward crisis management, accountability and the prevention of further military escalation. 

The developing crisis demonstrated the consequences of foreign military involvement when regional tensions are involved with international alliances and weapons transfers. 


















EXPELLED! CHINA FACES UNHRC FURY OVER C-11 EXPERIMENTS! 
-By Anishka Kuikel, Sambridhi Phuyal, and Kavin Pant, Chief Reporters of UNHRC
Kathmandu, 25 August 

The United Nations Human Rights Council voted to temporarily expel China on 25 August 2026. The move followed explosive allegations of forced recruitment, human trafficking, and international involvement in a secret human experimentation initiative.
The session originally opened on civilian protection, with the delegation of Pakistan, represented by Ojaswi Pant questioning the delegate of the United States, represented by  Sarjan Lamichhane over global arms transfers. Debate quickly pivoted when Chinese representative, Samyog Sedhai, was forced to defend domestic detention programs as socio-economic counter-extremism.
Chaos erupted after crisis intelligence linked China and the CIA Pentagon to Project C-11, a joint operation accused of trafficking subjects to sub-Saharan Africa for DNA extraction. The crisis deepened when the FBI seized 83 terabytes of CIA data, splitting the United States into two factions, the Original USA and the CSA.
Member states immediately condemned the operation. The delegate of the United States declared, "These people are not being taken by will, they are being taken by force!"                            The delegate of Indonesia represented by Nirman Ghimire added a harsh critique, stating, "Even a dog can differentiate between meat and biscuits, but China can’t even differentiate between what is wrong and what is right."
The delegate of China defended the project, arguing that genetic enhancements could safely advance global medical research. The delegation also highlighted perceived Western hypocrisy, questioning why United States funding toward foreign conflicts raised no outcry while Chinese scientific progress drew international outrage.
The delegate of Syria briefly defended human enhancement in principle, triggering a heated interjection against the United States delegation. Following the intense debate, the council voted by majority to remove China from the assembly.
The delegate of China has since requested re-entry through the DISEC President after issuing a formal apology, leaving Project C-11 under active committee investigation.

BLAME GAME ERUPTS! ECOSOC BATTLES OVER SANCTIONS 

By Alishka Kuikel, Shreni Chapagain, and Divyendu Subedi, Reporters of ECOSOC
Kathmandu , August 25

Debate on humanitarian exemptions prompted accusations over who bears responsibility for "The impact of trade sanctions, blockades and economic restrictions on poverty-stricken nations," with discussions focusing on how humanitarian exemptions can protect access to essential healthcare, medicines, and aid while maintaining economic measures.
The debate intensified following news that the United States, represented by Ayushman Dahal, had imposed new sanctions, restricting oil exports, banking access, shipping, and essential goods on Iran and the Democratic People's Republic of Korea (DPRK).
"The missiles and oil shipments of DPRK should be subjected to inspections," claimed the delegate of the USA, urging the need for the measures for regional security.
Mikel Shrestha, the delegate of Iran, quickly turned the conversation toward accountability, questioning how the USA would implement humanitarian exemptions in real life, and not just on paper.
In turn, the USA delegate shifted the attention to the delegate of China, Manushree Adhikari Chandani, inquiring, "How can China appeal for humanitarian aid while its own conduct includes unlawful orders like property theft and illegal military operations?"
As the USA deflected accusations, Afghanistan's delegate, Nickson Shahi, pushed back, questioning why the USA couldn't answer for its own role in sanctions that have deepened poverty across developing nations, Afghanistan among them, a country already struggling under Taliban rule.
Following a crisis update, the committee grew notably quiet, prompting the Secretary-General to note that despite "so much research," delegates don't speak up, urging them to respond more actively.
The silence was broken when the committee descended into back-and-forth arguments among delegates, circling who should give up their weapons and arms to ensure peace. The USA delegate, Ayushman Dahal, declared, "Until Iran surrenders its uranium and nuclear weapons, the USA will not remove any of the sanctions put on them." The delegate also urged other nations to drop their weapons.
The DPRK delegate, portrayed by Roneev Udas, responded, "If the USA wants Korea to hand over missiles, the USA should also give up its own missiles." The delegation pointed out the USA wasn’t innocent either.
Discussions came to a close as delegates settled on the measures required to reduce the impact of sanctions and economic restrictions on vulnerable populations. Further negotiations are expected to determine the committee’s position on these measures.

















PLASTIC, POWER & BETRAYAL: UNEP DESCENDS INTO A GLOBAL ENVIRONMENTAL WAR
-By Ranish Mahat, Chief Reporter of UNEP
Kathmandu, 25 August

Tensions ran high in the United Nations Environment Programme (UNEP) during the second session of TESMUN 2026, as delegates transitioned from debating microplastic pollution to managing a full-scale international environmental crisis. 
The General Speakers List opened with statement proposals from China, the United States, Iran, Nepal, and Russia focusing on microplastic degradation across developing nations. The UAE outlined financial assistance frameworks, while Afghanistan proposed strict bans on single-use plastic bags and Bangladesh highlighted severe microplastic contamination in its river systems. 
The Dais Panel warned the committee, “Delegates, please avoid reading directly from papers and always have a proper source of information.”
The discussion became more tense when the delegate of Germany, Yudin Thapa Magar, claimed he was helping reduce plastic leakage, a statement challenged by the delegate of Brazil ,Subhatmika Ghimire. A separate dispute between the delegate of Kenya, Subrat Khadka and the delegate of Nepal, Pranzole Mahara Chhetri, led to the suspension of Nepal for unparliamentary behavior. The delegate of Germany was later suspended for misbehavior as well.
The committee erupted into chaos following a sudden crisis update reporting widespread ecological damage. Toxic algal blooms, plummeting fish populations, and severe threats to coastal food supplies left up to 100 million people facing acute food and water insecurity. 
During an emergency presidential address, India directly accused the United States of fueling the crisis, prompting the U.S. delegation to demand verifiable proof.
The United Kingdom cautioned against making unsubstantiated claims, while Iran warned of an impending humanitarian crisis. In response, both the United States and China pledged support for emergency aid.
The conflict soon escalated into a formal courtroom trial. Seeking immunity, Iran claimed it was the primary provider of relief aid and accused China and the UK of driving the environmental disaster. While acknowledging Iran’s relief efforts, the UK maintained Iran shared responsibility, while China dismissed claims that other nations could not assist. Tensions peaked when China accused Russia of delaying aid, prompting Bangladesh to interject: "If China is really taking accountability, why did China start this crisis?"
Disruptions escalated when the President of Bangladesh condemned the mistreatment of developing nations, prompting Chair Rujal Ojha to call for order. Meanwhile, the President of the United States questioned Iran’s accusations against China.
Taking the stand, Iran’s Supreme Leader argued that China, as a global superpower, should carry greater accountability. A key witness was also presented to support Iran’s position.
The trial took a critical turn when the Dais Panel unveiled a declassified report linking Russia to covert plastic dumping across India and Bangladesh. The report revealed that the operation aimed to sabotage regional food and water security, forcing reliance on Russian filtration technology and agricultural imports. Intelligence further alleged that Russia pressured Iran to take the fall while mounting a global disinformation campaign.
With the environmental crisis deepening, the committee launched an immediate investigation into Russia’s liability.
