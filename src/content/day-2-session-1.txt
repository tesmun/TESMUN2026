MELTING GLACIERS! RISING ACCUSATIONS! UNEP DESCENDS INTO CHAOS! 
-Ranish Mahat, Chief Reporter of UNEP

An escalating glacier crisis turned the UNEP committee into a diplomatic battleground as the delegate of China faced allegations of secret geoengineering, Iran was accused of providing technological assistance, and countries began shifting alliances. 
The crisis began when a report announced massive glacier loss in the Pamir-Karakoram region, known as the “Roof of the World.” Around 3.2 billion cubic litres of water were reportedly released, threatening millions across China, Pakistan and surrounding countries. The delegate of the United States, Sejal Adhikari, then accused the delegate of China, Adhrit Regmi, of secretly operating“Project White Summit”, an alleged geoengineering programme using aerosols to influence monsoon patterns and accelerate glacier melting. The delegate of China denied the allegations and argued that it was being unfairly blamed. 
The delegate of Iran, Bishesh Bhatt was subsequently accused of developing technology that could have assisted China. Iran rejected the claims, warning that it could suspend environmental cooperation in the Strait of Hormuz, and claimed that it had been unfairly targeted.
The crisis widened when reports claimed Russia’s Vostok ice shelf had released around 48 billion tons of ice, threatening Arctic infrastructure. Malaysian ports also faced risks, while tensions around the Strait of Hormuz raised concerns over energy supplies and oil prices.
The biggest diplomatic shift came when the delegate of Iran announced that it was distancing itself from the delegate of China and the delegate of Russia, Supreme Chapagain while seeking closer cooperation with the delegate of  the United States and European countries. Russia later announced financial assistance for affected countries, while criticizing Western media for what it called misleading reports.
Amid the growing accusations, the Dais expressed frustration with the committee's response. “Delegates, this is UNEP. We are here to solve a crisis, not simply describe one,” said Moderator Unnat Aryal.
The session ended with continued divisions over responsibility and limited cooperation. Vice-Chair Sankalpa Neupane stated, “We witnessed strong disagreements today, but disagreement should be the beginning of negotiation, not the end of cooperation.”






























BROKEN PROMISES! BILLIONS MISSING! ECOSOC DEMANDS ACCOUNTABILITY! 
By Alishka Kuikel and Shreni Chapagain, Chief Reporters of ECOSOC
Kathmandu, Aug 18, 2026
As the global financial gap continues to threaten the Sustainable Development Goals (SDGs), delegates in the Economic and Social Council (ECOSOC) engaged in a heated debate Tuesday over equitable burden-sharing and international aid commitments. 
The discussion focused on the financial contributions and commitments of major economies. The delegate of Iran, Mikel Shrestha, questioned the representative of the United States, Ayushman Dahal, regarding international development assistance.
“Why did the delegate of the USA decrease the Official Development Assistance (ODA) by 56.9% when the USA has the largest economy in the world?” the delegate of Iran asked, questioning where U.S. support was during global crises. 
The delegate of Russia, Shaanvi Shrestha, also questioned the commitment of developed nations, asking why major economies had reduced support and failed to fulfill international funding pledges. Delegates from Iran and Russia subsequently criticized the United States regarding its financial accountability and inclusion on international monitoring lists.
In response to the growing financing gap, several representatives called for structured burden-sharing systems to support the SDGs, leading nations to form aligned blocs around shared development positions.
The committee later shifted focus following a crisis update, as representatives of Iran, North Korea (Roneev Udas), and the United States addressed allegations regarding unrecorded financial transfers. The delegate of the United States rejected the claims during debate.
“There is no complete evidence released supporting the claims,” the delegate of the United States stated.
The delegate of Russia opposed the allegations, arguing that the claims were intended to disrupt relations between allied states, while the delegate of the United States called for the verification of evidence.
Addressing the broader economic strain, Selina Kandel, the delegate of South Africa, called for formal, transparent agreements, stating, "Achieving the SDGs requires funding models capable of supporting long-term development." 
As a secondary crisis update commenced, the delegate of the United States called for an end to mutual accusations, issued an apology to affected nations, and announced plans to offer low-interest loans to developing countries.
Despite the announcement, the delegate of Iran maintained pressure regarding historical accountability, asking whether institutional bias against Iran remained. Following the exchange, delegates began organizing into regional blocs to draft formal resolutions on transparent ODA tracking and equitable SDG financing models.

18th August 2026 
-By Osang Ghising, Chief Reporter of DISEC 
A false-flag arms plot involving intercepted heavy weaponry and military drones triggered an international crisis at the TESMUN 2026 DISEC committee Tuesday, before a recovered hard drive exposed an alleged British intelligence scheme to frame world powers. 
The crisis began when the Italian Navy intercepted a vessel carrying 1,200 assault rifles with erased serial numbers, 150 machine guns, 60 anti-armor systems and 40 military drones. Documents identified France as the supplier and Iraq as the destination; however, the delegate of Iraq, Sukson Khadka, denied ordering the cargo, while Mossad intelligence alleged the arsenal was intended for attacks against Israel. 
The discovery prompted immediate diplomatic fallout. The delegate of the United States, Aashima Bhattarai, severed diplomatic ties with France, while the delegate of Israel, Saya Luitel, launched airstrikes inside Iraq. As the delegates of Russia (Sabhya Devkota) and China (Sadikshya Bhattarai) called for a ceasefire, the delegate of France, Nensee Syangbo Tamang, accepted responsibility for the shipment following intense pressure and threats to reduce the Group of Seven to a Group of Six. 
The investigation took an unexpected turn when files recovered from the vessel suggested that Iraq had allegedly been used as a false destination. Further evidence implicated an official connected to British intelligence in an operation allegedly designed to increase regional conflict and frame other powers. The delegate of the United Kingdom, Soham Paudel, firmly rejected the allegation.
The revelations changed the direction of the committee, raising questions over whether the intercepted weapons were part of a genuine arms transfer or an attempt to manufacture an international crisis. What initially appeared to be an Iraqi weapons threat had instead become a dispute over the reliability of intelligence and the consequences of acting on unverified evidence.
As the plot was exposed, the committee shifted away from accusations and toward cooperation, with delegates supporting stronger measures to prevent illicit arms transfers and improve international weapons monitoring. This unified stance marks a decisive step toward restoring stability in a region pushed to the brink.

18 August 2026  
An illicit arms shipment intercepted by Italy sparked a rapidly escalating international crisis in the DISEC committee Tuesday, eventually uncovering alleged shell companies, erased weapon serial numbers and a covert network accused of artificially increasing regional demand for weapons.
The crisis began when Italian authorities seized a vessel carrying weapons from France destined for Iraq. The delegate of Iraq, Sukson Khadka, denied ordering the cargo, while several weapons were found with erased serial numbers. Following intense pressure from the delegates of Israel (Saya Luitel), Russia (Sabhya Devkota), and the United States (Aashima Bhattarai), the delegate of France, Nensee Syangbo Tamang, accepted responsibility for the shipment. 
The situation escalated when the delegate of Israel launched unilateral strikes against suspected weapons facilities in Iraq. Russia and China condemned the strikes, arguing that unilateral military action would further destabilize the region.
A multinational investigation subsequently uncovered a wider supply network. Italian authorities traced component parts to defense sales involving China and Pakistan, while encrypted data revealed shell companies using forged documents to conceal the weapons' final destinations. 
The investigation reached its most critical point when leaked files allegedly implicated a British intelligence-affiliated official in operating the network and artificially inflating regional arms demand. The delegate of the United Kingdom, Soham Paudel, firmly rejected the allegations, calling them fabricated.
Vice-Chair Rijan Shrestha urged delegates to move beyond accusations, stating, “We have heard the accusations. Now the committee needs to hear solutions.”
The developments left the committee facing questions beyond the intercepted shipment, as delegates were forced to consider whether illicit arms transfers were being driven by covert networks seeking to profit from regional instability.
What began as a single intercepted vessel in Italy has exposed a global Web of intelligence failures, shell companies, and state-level accusations. As DISEC enters its next session, all eyes are on the committee to see whether delegates can unite to enforce transparency or remain deadlocked by diplomatic denials.

FAKE! FRAUD! FRENZY! AI VIDEOS RATTLE THE NATIONAL PARLIAMENT! 
-Aarush Katuwal, Chief reporter of LP I
18 August


An expert forensic report has confirmed that videos and documents presented as evidence during the day’s simulation of the national parliament session were generated or manipulated using artificial intelligence. Delegates were left to debate a political crisis that—according to the subsequent investigation—had not actually occurred.
The crisis began after a video was published claiming that former Prime Minister K.P. Sharma Oli and Sher Bahadur Deuba had returned to Kathmandu to form a political alliance. The authenticity of the video could not be verified, yet delegates presented it on the parliamentary floor, prompting immediate discussion over the alleged political development.
The delegate of Balendra Shah, Manjul Pratap Ghimire, responded to the accusations, stating, “The Government of Nepal will not allow any misinformation to destabilize Nepal, but political expression will continue to be free in Nepal.” The opposition rejected the delegate’s response, accusing the government of attempting to restrict freedom of speech.
The crisis escalated when further information claimed that the delegate of Balendra Shah had ended his political relationship with the delegate of Ravi Lamichhane, Prasanna Puri, and that the Nepali Congress had split into two factions. Despite the lack of verification, delegates from the Special Speakers List questioned the delegate of Balendra Shah about the developments, with one delegate even asking whether the original video could be obtained.
By noon, the number of alleged videos and documents had increased, with new claims repeatedly contradicting earlier reports. 
Due to the delegates’ lack of verification for their claims, the Chair, Saksham Thapaliya, informed the delegates by saying, “Freedom of expression does not mean freedom from responsibility. Every delegate must consider the consequences of the information they present.”
The investigation took a significant turn when a digital-forensics expert confirmed that the videos had been manipulated, although the origin of the material could not be identified. An emergency forensic report issued at 1:00 PM subsequently confirmed that the reported political alliances, betrayal and split within the Nepali Congress were fabricated.
The Secretary-General, Dibas Khadka, informed delegates that the crisis was intended to test their ability to think quickly rather than their research skills, describing it as “a national issue requiring an immediate parliamentary response.” Then, the Secretary-General later participated temporarily in the Special Speakers List as the shadow delegate of K.P. Sharma Oli, describing the ongoing situation of the parliament parties as “Amoebas continuously splitting and recombining.” 
The chair and the secretary-general expressed their dissatisfaction, since some delegates, in response to these developments, beat their desks rather than asking for a point of order. 
The incident left the committee facing a broader question over how parliamentarians should verify information before acting on it, particularly when AI-generated material can rapidly create the appearance of a political crisis.
