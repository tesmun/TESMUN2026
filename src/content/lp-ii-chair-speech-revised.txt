"Our greatest glory is not in never falling, but in rising every time we fall." — Confucius

Warm greetings to the Chairperson of the Programme and Secretary General, Mr. Dibas Khadka; Chief Guest, Honourable Managing Director, Mr. Ram Basnet; Special Guest, Chairperson Madam Sheetal Rana; Principal Mr. Prashant Rajak; Administrative In-charge Mr. Niraj Bikram Rana; MUN Advisor Mr. Govinda Gautam; esteemed members of the Secretariat and Organizing Committee; fellow dais members; and my dear delegates.

It is an absolute honour to stand before you today as the Chairperson of Model Parliament II, not merely to close a committee, but to reflect upon a journey that none of us will forget.

When I first saw my delegates enter the committee, my first impression was exceptional. I saw enthusiasm, confidence, and a willingness to participate. I believed from the beginning that MP II had the potential to become something remarkable.

However, the journey did not remain perfect. As the sessions progressed, there was a gradual decline in the performance of the delegates. The debates became less engaging, participation began to fall, and at one point even the Secretary General was disappointed with the direction in which the committee was heading. Every one of us had high expectations after the delegates’ remarkable performance in the first session.

But sometimes, the greatest achievements come after the greatest doubts.

And then came yesterday. Yesterday was, without a doubt, an exceptional day for Model Parliament II. The committee came alive. The room was filled with arguments, questions, counterarguments, negotiations, and, most importantly, thought. The delegates demonstrated that they were not simply speaking for the sake of speaking; they were analysing issues, challenging one another, and genuinely engaging with the agenda.

MP II became one of the most active, thoughtful, and memorable committees of this conference. Perhaps most importantly, my delegates proved the Secretary General wrong.

What made this journey even more exciting were the individuals who gave MP II its character. Nischal Rai kept trying, even when he was wrong. Suhang Nembang never lost hope, even when he did not receive the majority of votes. Barshaman Pun brought the loudest voice in the entire committee. Balen Shah impressed us with his exceptional research, while Rabi Lamichhane somehow found a way to speak logically in every situation possible. These moments, big and small, made the journey more exciting and memorable.

As a Chair, my experience in MP II has been the best experience I could have wished for. It taught me that chairing is not simply about controlling a committee or maintaining decorum. It is about watching delegates grow, challenging them when they fall short, giving them opportunities to improve, and ultimately seeing them exceed expectations.

To my delegates, thank you for the arguments, disagreements, laughter, chaos, and, most importantly, for giving this committee its identity. To the entire Secretariat, my fellow dais members, and the committee in-charges, thank you for making this journey meaningful.

As we close this conference, I want to leave you with one thought: a great delegate is not the one who speaks the most. A great delegate is the one who makes people think. A great conference is not remembered for how it began, but for how it ended.

MP II may have had its setbacks, but it ended by proving what it was capable of. We did not simply conclude a committee; we proved that expectations can be exceeded, doubts can be defeated, and a decline can become a comeback.

With that, I congratulate all the participants of TESMUN 2026 and wish each and every one of you success beyond these conference walls.
