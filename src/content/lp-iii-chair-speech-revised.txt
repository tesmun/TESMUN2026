"Great journeys are not defined by where they begin, but by how far they take us."

A very warm good morning to the Honourable Secretary General of TESMUN 2026, Mr. Dibas Khadka; our Honourable Chief Guest, Mr. Ram Basnet; our Special Guest, Madam Sheetal Rana; Respected Principal, Mr. Prashant Rajak; Administrative In-charge, Mr. Niraj Bikram Rana; our MUN Advisor, Mr. Govinda Gautam; distinguished members of the Secretariat and Organizing Committee; my fellow dais members; and, most importantly, the delegates.

Standing here today feels a little strange. Not because the conference is ending, but because two years ago I was sitting on the other side of this room. I was a first-time delegate in Model Parliament III, representing Upendra Yadav, and somehow ended that conference with an Outstanding Delegate award.

The following year, I returned to MUN in DISEC as the delegate of China and was fortunate enough to receive Outstanding Delegate once again. Back then, I thought I had figured MUN out: research, speeches, points of information, arguments, negotiations, and trying to get your name called during the awards.

But there was one thing I never imagined. I never imagined that two years later I would return to the same committee, not as a delegate, but as its Chair. That is the most special part of this entire experience. The room is the same, and the committee is the same, but the person sitting here is very different.

And then there were all of you. From the very beginning, MP III made one thing clear: this was never going to be a quiet committee.

Harka Raj Rai deserves credit for establishing that very early. Whenever something happened that he did not agree with, there was always a point of order waiting around the corner. Even after being suspended in the SSL, Harka Raj Rai somehow found a way to continue participating by speaking to the committee through the window. That was the first time I saw a delegate turn a suspension into a change of venue. Suhang Nembang carried the whole CPN-UML with her defensive points.

Then there was Khusbu Oli, who somehow became our unofficial timekeeper. At some point, I was no longer sure whether I was chairing the committee or whether Khusbu was reminding me how much time remained for the GSL.

Then came Rabi Lamichhane. The accusations were so numerous that at one point the delegate simply said, "I was in jail."

Some of you, especially the delegate of Gyan Bahadur Shahi, seemed prepared to disagree with absolutely everyone in the committee.

Then we had Manish Jha. Some people participate in a committee by raising their placard. Manish participated with the entire room: the table banging, the clapping, the shouting, the energy, and the sheer volume. Honestly, that energy mattered because Manish had a way of making the room feel more alive.

There was also yesterday’s crisis. I do not think anyone expected the personal assistant of Swornim Wagle to turn a crisis update into an acting performance. And how could I forget the crisis that had this committee questioning the entire political landscape of Nepal, when Balendra Shah was overthrown as Prime Minister and Pushpa Kamal Dahal was suddenly re-elected as the new Prime Minister?

As a delegate, you experience MUN from your own seat. You remember your speeches, arguments, mistakes, and awards. As a Chair, you get to watch the entire room. You see the nervous delegate become confident, someone who barely spoke suddenly challenge the strongest speaker, and alliances, disagreements, and negotiations unfold every minute.

Being a Chair is not always easy. There were times when I had to stop you, remind you about procedure, and ask you to lower your voices. I also remember the times when I had to ask you to lower your placards because sixteen points of information were raised during a single GSL speech. That shows how active MP III was throughout every session.

To my delegates, remember that MUN is not about proving that you are the smartest person in the room. It is about being willing to contribute something to the room. Stand up, speak with confidence, listen to someone who disagrees with you, and have the courage to try again when things do not go your way. Some of the most important things you gain from MUN will never be printed on a certificate.

To my fellow dais members, thank you for being part of this journey. I also extend my sincere gratitude to the Press, Logistics, and Cartoonist teams, the committee in-charge, and everyone working behind the scenes for giving us the platform to create these memories.

Delegates, after everything we have debated, questioned, defended, challenged, and somehow survived together, it has been an absolute honour to serve as your Chair.
