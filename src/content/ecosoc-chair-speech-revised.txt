A very warm good morning to the Respected Secretary General of this conference, Mr. Dibas Khadka; Honourable Managing Director and Chief Guest, Mr. Ram Basnet; Honourable Chairperson and Special Guest, Madam Sheetal Rana; Respected Principal, Mr. Prashant Rajak; Administrative In-charge, Mr. Niraj Bikram Rana; MUN Advisor, Mr. Govinda Gautam; distinguished members of the Executive Board and Organizing Committee; delegates; and everyone present here today.

Three years ago, I walked into my first MUN conference as a delegate, with very little idea of what MUN would eventually become for me. Today, standing here as the Chair of ECOSOC, I find myself looking back at how much has changed since that first conference.

My TESMUN journey began in Grade 8, in the Legislative Parliament, where I represented Ramesh Lekhak and received a Special Mention. The following year, I returned to ECOSOC as the delegate of China and was fortunate enough to receive the Best Delegate award.

At that point, MUN was mostly about preparation: researching my country, preparing speeches, finding arguments, and trying to perform better with every conference. But becoming a Chair changed my perspective completely.

As a delegate, you experience a committee from inside it. As a Chair, you see the entire committee come together: the speeches, negotiations, arguments, alliances, and unexpected moments.

This year, I had the privilege of chairing ECOSOC alongside my Vice Chair, Sima Thapa, and our Moderator, Kunjal Karki. I genuinely could not have asked for a better team.

Of course, chairing a committee comes with responsibilities: maintaining decorum, keeping debate moving, managing time, and making sure everyone gets an opportunity to participate. Somewhere between all of that, there were moments that made this committee unforgettable.

One of those moments was when Pakistan and Afghanistan somehow decided that the best solution to their differences was to create a new country together, which they proudly named "Pakisghan." Another moment came during the crisis, when Iranian oil was allegedly stolen by the USA and the delegate of Iran began crying and having a mental breakdown in front of the committee because the oil had been stolen.

As a Chair, you are supposed to remain neutral and composed, but moments like that make it incredibly difficult to keep a straight face.

Beyond the jokes and chaos, what I will remember most is the enthusiasm of the delegates. Watching people who may have been nervous at the beginning stand up and confidently present their ideas was genuinely rewarding.

Being a Chair also taught me that MUN is not about having the perfect speech or knowing every single fact. It is about being willing to listen, adapt, negotiate, and accept that the best argument in the room might not be your own.

To the delegates of ECOSOC, I hope you take more than awards from this conference. Take the confidence to speak up, the ability to listen to people you disagree with, and the memories you made along the way.

To my Vice Chair, Sima Thapa, and Moderator, Kunjal Karki, thank you for making this experience easier, more enjoyable, and, at times, more chaotic than I expected. Finally, I thank the Executive Board, the Organizing Committee, and everyone who worked behind the scenes to make TESMUN 2026 possible.

Thank you, and congratulations to everyone who made TESMUN 2026 a success.
